# AI Across Science, Energy, Aerospace and Physical Systems

**Retrospective:** January 27–July 27, 2026
**Outlook:** August 2026–January 2028
**Domains:** biotechnology, nanotechnology, advanced materials, energy, aerospace, robotics and multimodal sim-to-real agents

## Executive conclusion

Capital and technical talent are moving from **software-only generative AI** into two broader categories:

1. **AI for science:** models, agents and autonomous laboratories that discover molecules, materials, catalysts and biological mechanisms.
2. **Physical AI:** multimodal models that perceive, reason and act through robots, vehicles, drones, scientific instruments and industrial equipment.

The highest-growth opportunities are not isolated within one industry. They occur where several domains converge:

| High-growth intersection                                      |      Commercial pull |         Technical maturity |      Near-term demand |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------: | -------------------------: | --------------------: |
| AI materials discovery × batteries, semiconductors and energy |            Very high |                     Medium |             Very high |
| Robotics × industrial energy inspection and maintenance       |                 High | High for constrained tasks |             Very high |
| Aerospace autonomy × multimodal agents × sim-to-real          |            Very high |                Medium-high |             Very high |
| Autonomous laboratories × scientific agents                   |                 High |                     Medium | High and accelerating |
| Energy digital twins × AI control × real-time systems         |            Very high |                Medium-high |                  High |
| World models × robot foundation models × fleet learning       | Potentially enormous |               Early-medium |        Rapidly rising |
| Advanced materials × aerospace thermal and structural systems |                 High |                     Medium |                Rising |
| AI data centers × power systems × cooling materials           |       Extremely high |                       High |             Immediate |

The central market transition is:

> **From models that generate information to systems that discover, manufacture, control and operate physical assets.**

The strongest demand is emerging for engineers who can combine ML infrastructure with simulation, scientific data, real-time execution, distributed systems and safety-critical validation.

# 2. Bio, nanotechnology and advanced materials

## Primary AI applications

### Molecular and biological design

AI systems are increasingly used for:

* Protein structure and complex prediction.
* Protein, antibody and enzyme design.
* Ligand generation and molecular docking.
* Toxicity, pharmacokinetics and metabolic prediction.
* Genomics, single-cell analysis and target identification.
* Patient and disease-state representation.
* Laboratory-protocol generation and experiment prioritization.

The market is moving beyond static protein-structure prediction toward models that represent interactions among proteins, ligands, cells, biological pathways and experimental conditions.

### Nanotechnology and materials

Important applications include:

* Crystal and molecular property prediction.
* Generative inverse design: specify a required property and generate candidate materials.
* Interatomic potentials that approximate expensive quantum calculations.
* Battery-electrode and electrolyte discovery.
* Catalyst, membrane and sorbent design.
* Semiconductor, photonic and magnetic materials.
* Polymers, composites, coatings and surface engineering.
* Process-property relationships for synthesis and manufacturing.
* Automated microscopy, spectroscopy and diffraction interpretation.

### Self-driving laboratories

A self-driving laboratory connects:

1. A scientific hypothesis or design model.
2. Candidate-generation and optimization algorithms.
3. Robotic synthesis or sample preparation.
4. Automated characterization.
5. Result interpretation.
6. Selection of the next experiment.

This is one of the most important intersections between scientific AI, robotics and multimodal agents. The constraint is shifting from “Can the model propose a candidate?” to “Can the system manufacture, measure and validate it reproducibly?”

## Last-six-month direction

The Isomorphic and CuspAI rounds illustrate a broader move toward **full-stack scientific discovery companies** rather than standalone predictive models. Toyota Research Institute is recruiting across machine learning, robotics, X-ray diffraction analysis and automated materials workflows, demonstrating how industrial research teams are merging software, ML and laboratory science. ([Lever][9])

The most investable advanced-materials targets have direct industrial pull:

* Battery performance and safety.
* Lower-energy semiconductor manufacturing.
* Data-center cooling and thermal management.
* Carbon capture and gas separation.
* Catalysis for hydrogen, ammonia and sustainable fuels.
* Lightweight and high-temperature aerospace components.
* Advanced magnets and power electronics.

## Market limitation

Scientific discovery is not equivalent to software generation. Candidate generation may become very fast while experimental validation, scale-up, regulatory approval and manufacturing remain slow.

The commercially valuable infrastructure therefore includes:

* Uncertainty calibration.
* Data provenance.
* Experiment lineage.
* Failed-experiment capture.
* Reproducibility.
* Human approval.
* Model-to-laboratory interfaces.
* Scale-up and manufacturability prediction.

# 3. Energy

AI has a dual relationship with energy:

1. It is creating a major new source of electricity demand.
2. It is becoming a tool for operating and expanding the energy system.

The International Energy Agency estimates that global data-center electricity consumption could increase from approximately 415 TWh in 2024 to about 945 TWh in 2030. The United States and China account for most of the projected growth. ([IEA][10])

### Power-grid forecasting and control

* Load and renewable-production forecasting.
* Congestion prediction.
* Dynamic line-rating estimation.
* Grid-topology optimization.
* Contingency analysis.
* Demand response.
* Battery dispatch.
* Electricity-market bidding.
* Outage and wildfire-risk prediction.

Reinforcement learning and optimization can help grid operators explore corrective actions, but deployment remains constrained by safety, explainability and regulatory approval.

### Generation and industrial assets

AI is applied to:

* Predictive maintenance.
* Nuclear plant monitoring.
* Geothermal exploration.

Current fusion job descriptions already request combinations of AI-assisted model-predictive control, reinforcement learning in simulation, anomaly detection, real-time state estimation and digital twins. ([Lever][11])

### Energy materials

This is one of the strongest intersections in the entire analysis:

* Battery electrodes and electrolytes.

* Fuel-cell catalysts.
* Hydrogen electrolysis.

* High-temperature superconductors.

A new infrastructure category is emerging around:

* Power-aware workload scheduling.

This creates roles that combine ML infrastructure, distributed scheduling and power-system knowledge.

> **Prototype autonomy → validated platform → production capacity → recurring mission deployment.**

## Hiring constraint

The sector must compete with AI laboratories for scarce software and ML talent while also facing an aging aerospace workforce. Reuters reported roughly 10,000 specialized positions going unfilled annually in the UK sector, with more than half of licensed aerospace engineers over 50. ([Reuters][13])

That talent gap increases demand for engineers who can bridge modern AI with traditional aerospace assurance, controls and systems engineering.

# 5. Robotics and physical AI

## Market transition

The robotics market is moving through four stages:

1. **Programmed automation:** fixed industrial cells.
2. **Perception-driven automation:** robots adapt to variations in known tasks.
3. **Learning-enabled robots:** policies are learned from demonstrations or reinforcement learning.
4. **Physical foundation models:** multimodal systems generalize across tasks, environments and embodiments.

Most current commercial value is still concentrated in stages two and three. Stage four is attracting exceptional funding because it promises reusable intelligence across many robots.

## What accelerated during the last six months

### Robot foundation models

Vision-language-action models translate images, language and robot state into actions. Current research focuses on:

* Cross-robot training.
* Large-scale behavior models.
* Diffusion policies.
* Learning from human video.
* Tactile and force inputs.
* Long-horizon planning.
* Online adaptation.

Current Toyota Research Institute roles cover world foundation models, large behavior models, diffusion policies, simulation and multimodal learning from RGB, depth, flow, semantics, tactile and audio data. ([Lever][14])

### Data has become a market

The robotics bottleneck is increasingly real-world interaction data rather than model architecture alone. Capital is flowing into:

* Teleoperation systems.
* Demonstration collection.
* Data normalization.
* Cross-embodiment datasets.
* Failure and recovery trajectories.
* Fleet-learning platforms.
* Synthetic-data generation.

### Industrial deployment is winning before general-purpose autonomy

The highest near-term demand is for robots that operate in bounded but economically important environments:

* Warehouses and logistics.
* Automotive and electronics manufacturing.
* Energy-facility inspection.
* Construction.
* Mining.
* Agriculture.
* Pharmaceutical and laboratory automation.
* Defense and hazardous environments.

ANYbotics, for example, is recruiting for reinforcement-learning and sim-to-real work applied to mission-critical inspection in energy, process and utility facilities. ([Lever][15])

# 6. Multimodal agents and sim-to-real

## Terminology

**World model**
A learned representation that predicts how an environment may evolve after an action.

**Vision-language-action model, or VLA**
A model that consumes visual observations and language instructions and generates robot actions.

**Sim-to-real**
Training or testing a policy in simulation and transferring it to physical hardware.

**Real-to-sim**
Reconstructing a real environment, asset or task inside a simulator.

**Domain randomization**
Varying textures, lighting, friction, mass, sensor noise and other parameters so a policy does not overfit to one simulated world.

**System identification**
Estimating physical parameters of the real system to make the simulator more representative.

**Hardware-in-the-loop**
Running real controllers or compute hardware against a simulated vehicle or environment.

**Fleet learning**
Aggregating data and failures from deployed robots to improve shared models.

## Why multimodal agents matter

A physical agent may need to process:

* Cameras.
* Depth.
* Radar or lidar.
* Proprioception.
* Force and tactile signals.
* Audio.
* Maps.
* Equipment manuals.
* Operator language.
* Historical maintenance data.

The agent must then coordinate perception, reasoning, planning, tool use and low-level controllers.

This is substantially harder than a browser-based agent because errors can damage equipment or injure people.

## The real opportunity is the sim-to-real control plane

The most reusable platform is not necessarily a single robot model. It is the infrastructure that manages:

1. Scenario generation.
2. Synthetic-data production.
3. Policy training.
4. Simulation evaluation.
5. Stress and fault injection.
6. Hardware-in-the-loop testing.
7. Real-world canary deployment.
8. Telemetry collection.
9. Incident replay.
10. Retraining and release approval.

Current simulation roles explicitly describe closing the sim-to-real gap as a core engineering concern, while NVIDIA’s recent ICRA research emphasizes moving robot policies from simulation to physical systems. ([Ashby Jobs][16])

## Main unresolved problems

* Simulation fidelity does not guarantee real-world predictivity.
* Contact dynamics, deformable objects and friction remain difficult.
* Rare failures are poorly represented.
* Sensors age and become miscalibrated.
* Human behavior is difficult to simulate.
* Policies can exploit simulator defects.
* Long-horizon compounding errors are hard to measure.
* Safety evidence rarely transfers cleanly between embodiments.

Consequently, demand will grow for evaluation systems that measure not only simulated success, but the **correlation between simulated and real-world outcomes**.

# 8. Hiring trends

## The hybrid specialist is replacing the generic ML engineer

The most sought-after candidates increasingly combine three competencies:

1. **Domain knowledge:** biology, chemistry, materials, controls, energy or aerospace.
2. **AI knowledge:** multimodal models, graph networks, diffusion models, RL or scientific ML.
3. **Systems knowledge:** distributed training, data infrastructure, simulation, real-time software and production reliability.

Current role descriptions show this convergence clearly:

* TRI combines materials science, robotics, multimodal models and automated experimentation.

* Manufacturing volume.

### Likely technology developments

* Improved integration between simulation and fleet telemetry.
* Domain-specific scientific agents.
* Wider use of AI digital twins for energy and industrial control.
* Growing model deployment directly on edge devices.
* Better multimodal fusion of vision, force, audio and state.
* Increased use of synthetic data paired with smaller amounts of high-quality real data.

## Next 18 months: through January 2028

### Likely structural changes

* Vertically integrated physical-AI companies become increasingly dominant.
* Autonomous laboratories begin operating as shared discovery infrastructure.
* Large industrial companies establish internal physical-AI platforms.
* Robot intelligence becomes more separable from robot hardware, but not fully hardware-independent.
* Energy availability becomes an explicit constraint on AI product and infrastructure roadmaps.
* Scientific and physical-AI evaluation becomes a distinct enterprise-software category.
* Aerospace and defense adopt shorter development cycles built around simulation, continuous testing and software-defined platforms.
* Materials-discovery systems increasingly connect directly to process design and manufacturing constraints.

### Less certain but potentially transformative

* Large-scale commercial humanoid deployment outside highly structured facilities.
* Broad generalization of one policy across substantially different robot bodies.
* Mostly autonomous drug or materials discovery without intensive expert oversight.
* Fully autonomous energy-grid control.
* General-purpose aerospace agents operating outside tightly bounded mission envelopes.
